# Grill review – VERIFIED research synthesis

## Weakest claims

1. VERIFIED will improve decision quality in the offer phase.
   - Weak because the evidence shows need and design direction, not measured decision improvement.
   - The synthesis currently phrases this cautiously, but the underlying claim is still unproven.

2. Better tender-phase information will reduce building damage.
   - Weak because there is no direct causal chain from comparison method to fewer defects or lower insurance losses.
   - Finans Norge only supports problem magnitude, not causal effect.

3. Better documentation will improve lending or financing outcomes.
   - Weak because EBA, Billio and Kaza show associations and policy interest, not VERIFIED effect.

4. NOBB and EPD-Norge are sufficiently ready for decision support.
   - Weak because the current evidence mostly shows infrastructure existence, not completeness, quality or usability in tender practice.

5. One Click LCA and EC3 define the state of the art for the whole problem.
   - Weak because the tool comparisons come partly from local summaries and because each tool stops at a different point.

## Hidden assumptions

- That visible uncertainty will be understood by non-specialists rather than just noticed.
- That users will trust a comparison frame more when it is more explicit, even if it becomes more complex.
- That existing product data are available in enough quality for the cases VERIFIED will test.
- That a lighter method can still be rigorous enough for Forskningsrådet.
- That the combination of data, comparison and explanation is the true FoU gap, not one narrower subproblem.
- That the Norwegian offer phase can be generalised from contextual sources that are not directly behavioral studies.

## Missing perspectives

- Actual SMB contractors and estimators in Norwegian offer work.
- Purchasers / clients who receive the explanation and have to act on it.
- Product suppliers who control parts of the underlying documentation.
- A user-experience or human-factors perspective on whether the framework will actually be usable.
- A procurement/legal perspective on what can be claimed safely in offers and tender documents.
- A stronger independent view on NOBB and One Click LCA, beyond local summaries and vendor framing.

## False consensus

- The synthesis can look more settled than the source base really is on usability.
- It is easy to read the regulations, NOBB and EPD-Norge as if they already guarantee interoperability in practice; they do not.
- It is easy to read EC3 as if it solves the same problem as VERIFIED; it does not.
- It is easy to read agreement on data quality as agreement on a specific interface design; that leap is not supported.

## Overreliance

- Edelen 2018 carries a lot of the conceptual framing for data quality.
- The local state-of-the-art summary carries too much weight for Benke, Lohman, EC3, NOBB and One Click LCA.
- Mecca 2023 is used as a background method signal despite being backed by a secondary summary and a paywalled full text.
- The finance line leans on association rather than direct operational evidence.

## Causal overreach

- Correlation between energy efficiency and mortgage risk is not causation in this project context.
- Association between strong product data and better decisions is not proof that VERIFIED will improve decisions.
- Regulatory direction toward digital product passports is not proof that users will adopt or benefit from them in offers.
- Reporting that a tool has more integrated data is not proof that it leads to better user outcomes.

## Context problems

- Many sources are not Norwegian and not focused on SMB offer work.
- Benke is North America; EC3 and One Click LCA are vendor- or tool-contextual.
- The finance sources are lending-oriented, not tender-oriented.
- The damage statistics are aggregated and weather-skewed, not project-choice-specific.
- The NOBB and EPD evidence is infrastructural, not behavioural.
- The synthesis may still be too optimistic about transferability from general LCA/MCDA theory to the exact VERIFIED use case.

## Questions that would deepen the final text

1. What exact user action should VERIFIED change: shortlist, comparison, explanation, or final recommendation?
2. Which user is the primary one in the first pilot: contractor, buyer, or internal evaluator?
3. What is the minimum evidence standard for saying a result is “better” in the offer phase?
4. How much complexity can the user tolerate before the method becomes unusable?
5. Which data fields are truly available in the cases you want to test first?
6. What is the smallest set of alternatives that still demonstrates meaningful comparison?
7. What would count as failure of the method in the first pilot?
8. Should the text explicitly say that the project does not yet prove business or climate effect?
9. Which claims must be kept in the August version even if the evidence remains indirect?
10. Where do you want the strongest boundary line between background, hypothesis, and proven effect?
