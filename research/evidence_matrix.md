## Areas of agreement

The evidence consistently supports one conclusion: the current information landscape is fragmented, and VERIFIED should frame itself as a way to make existing data easier to use, compare and explain. Edelen 2018, CPR 2024, ESPR 2024, EPD-Norge and EBA EU 2023 all point in that direction. These sources are mostly independent of each other: one is methodological, two are EU regulation, one is an infrastructure explanation, and one is a financial policy report. Combined strength is high.

The evidence also agrees that role-based access and machine-readable structure matter. CPR 2024 and ESPR 2024 say this directly in regulatory form, while EPD-Norge describes EPDs as comparison tools and EBA EU 2023 shows that green-loan identification still varies in practice. The sources do not repeat the same evidence; they come from different sectors but converge on the same design requirement. Combined strength is high.

Another agreement is that SMBs and smaller actors face a more constrained operating environment. Nordic Council 2023 says LCA requirements are deliberately weaker for smaller actors; BKA2 and NFR 2026 show that the policy and project environment still treats this as an active problem; EBA NO 2023 argues that many good measures are possible, but only under specific conditions. The combined evidence is medium-high, because much of it is contextual or programmatic rather than direct user-behavior evidence.

## Areas of disagreement

There is a tension between the EBA NO 2023 guide’s claim that material changes can reduce emissions without extra cost and the more cautious sources that only support general information quality or regulatory direction. This is not a direct factual contradiction, because the 20% figure is context-specific and tied to one guide. But it should not be generalized into a blanket claim for VERIFIED. The guide is weaker than the regulation and methodological sources because it is a sector guide with context-sensitive assumptions.

There is also a methodological tension between wanting a simple overview and the fact that Edelen 2018 warns against collapsing data quality into a single score. CPR and ESPR push toward structured, searchable data, but they do not justify hiding uncertainty. This is a tension in interpretation and design, not in facts. It cannot be fully resolved; the safer reading is a two-layer interface: a short summary plus visible basis and limitations.

The finance sources are consistent on association, but not on causality. Kaza 2014 and Billio 2022 support lower mortgage risk for energy-efficient homes, while EBA EU 2023 is more about policy design and Finans Norge 2024 is about damage frequency and cost. None of them proves that a better tender-phase comparison tool will reduce losses. The disagreement is mostly about causal reach, and the stronger evidence is on association and policy need, not on project effect.

## State-of-the-art findings

The strongest well-established finding in the current set is that data quality and purpose-fit matter. That is supported by Edelen 2018 and is consistent with the regulatory move toward open, structured and interoperable product data in CPR 2024 and ESPR 2024.

The strongest emerging finding is that the building-data stack is being pushed toward digital product passports, searchable product information and role-based access. This is no longer speculative; it is visible in the 2024 EU regulations and in EPD-Norge’s explanation of EPDs as comparison-oriented documents.

The most plausible but still unverified claim is that a better comparison and explanation layer will improve tender decisions. The current evidence supports the need and the design direction, but not the effect on decisions, time use, or outcomes. That is exactly where VERIFIED’s FoU question still sits.

The unresolved question is not whether data should be clearer; it is how much structure is enough, for whom, and at what cost in the offer phase. The current sources do not answer that.

## Evidence gaps

- No direct empirical source in this set shows that VERIFIED improves tender quality, saves time, or changes chosen solutions.
- The most relevant method and benchmark sources still need opening or verification in full: Benke 2025, Lohman 2023, EC3, Mecca 2023, Weidema 1996, Ciroth 2016 and NOBB/One Click LCA independent sources.
- No current source in this set gives a Norwegian SMB field study of how offer-phase choices are actually made.
- No source yet gives a verified, independent NOBB primary source or a non-vendor view of the NOBB × One Click LCA link.
- No source yet gives a direct causal chain from better tender-phase information to fewer building defects or lower insurance losses.

## Most decision-relevant findings

1. VERIFIED should not claim a standard-setting role; it should claim a test of a comparison and explanation method.
   - Supported by CPR 2024, ESPR 2024, Edelen 2018, EBA EU 2023.

2. The main problem is not lack of data alone, but unusable, fragmented and context-mismatched data.
   - Supported by Edelen 2018, EPD-Norge, CPR 2024, ESPR 2024.

3. The solution must make role, access and uncertainty visible.
   - Supported by CPR 2024, ESPR 2024, Edelen 2018, EBA EU 2023.

4. Norwegian SMB context matters; the solution must stay light enough for offer-phase use.
   - Supported by Nordic Council 2023, BKA2, NFR 2026.

5. Financial and damage context can justify relevance, but not effect claims.
   - Supported by Billio 2022, Kaza 2014, EBA EU 2023, Finans Norge 2024.

6. Related initiatives show the field is active, but they do not remove the FoU gap.
   - Supported by BKA2, NOBB-OCL, EPD-Norge.
